################################################################################
1. PURPOSE, SCOPE, AND EMPLOYEE EMPOWERMENT
################################################################################

Generative Artificial Intelligence—including Large Language Models (LLMs), AI code assistants, conversational chat interfaces, autonomous agents, and synthetic media tools—represents a transformative capability that enhances workforce productivity, accelerates software engineering, and fosters creative problem-solving across modern technology enterprises.

Threshold Enterprise Systems encourages workforce members to leverage approved generative AI capabilities to innovate, automate routine administrative tasks, and optimize business workflows. However, the use of generative AI introduces significant organizational risks, including unintentional data leakage, copyright infringement, hallucinated inaccuracies, and cyber security vulnerabilities.

This Generative AI Usage Policy establishes mandatory rules, behavioral standards, and practical guardrails governing how all employees, managers, contractors, and interns utilize generative AI tools on behalf of Threshold Enterprise Systems.

This policy applies universally across all global operating locations in India, the United States, the United Kingdom, and Singapore.


################################################################################
2. APPROVED ENTERPRISE TOOLS VS. UNAPPROVED PUBLIC SERVICES
################################################################################

Threshold Enterprise Systems distinguishes strictly between corporate-sanctioned enterprise AI platforms and unapproved public consumer AI tools:

- Approved Corporate AI Services: Tools provisioned directly through enterprise single sign-on (SSO) with enterprise commercial agreements guaranteeing zero data retention, zero model training on corporate prompts, and encryption in transit and at rest. These include our internal Threshold Assistant, approved corporate GitHub Copilot Enterprise seats, and dedicated enterprise cloud endpoints.

- Unapproved Public Consumer Services: Free, public, or personal consumer accounts on third-party platforms (e.g., personal ChatGPT, personal Claude, Midjourney, public Hugging Face spaces). Use of unapproved consumer AI services for any company business is strictly prohibited.

- Third-Party Tool Procurement: Any new generative AI tool or browser extension must be submitted for formal vetting through the Enterprise Application Catalog and approved under AIG-003 prior to installation.


################################################################################
3. STRICTLY PROHIBITED INFORMATION IN PROMPTS
################################################################################

The protection of corporate intellectual property, client confidentiality, and employee privacy is absolute. Workforce members are strictly prohibited from inputting, pasting, uploading, or prompting any generative AI system with the following categories of information:

1. CONFIDENTIAL or RESTRICTED Data: Proprietary software source code, proprietary algorithms, model training weights, unannounced product roadmaps, trade secrets, and financial projections.

2. Customer and Client Data: Non-public customer datasets, confidential client contract terms, client transaction records, and proprietary client code bases.

3. Personally Identifiable Information (PII): Employee personal records (HR-007), payroll details, medical insurance records, national identity numbers, passwords, and cryptographic keys.

4. Legal and Regulatory Material: Privileged communications with legal counsel, pending litigation documents, and regulatory inquiry disclosures.

Only data classified as PUBLIC or non-sensitive, sanitized INTERNAL operational information may be processed using approved enterprise generative AI tools.


################################################################################
4. THE PRINCIPLE OF HUMAN ACCOUNTABILITY AND OUTPUT VERIFICATION
################################################################################

CRITICAL MANDATE: EMPLOYEES MUST NEVER ASSUME THAT AI-GENERATED OUTPUT IS ACCURATE, COMPLETE, UNBIASED, OR LAWFUL.

Generative AI models are statistical text prediction systems prone to 'hallucinations'—convincingly stated but entirely fabricated facts, fake legal citations, flawed algorithmic logic, and nonexistent software libraries.

Human Accountability Principle: The employee who utilizes generative AI output in their work product remains 100% personally accountable for the accuracy, quality, and integrity of that output. Blaming an AI model for an erroneous technical report, buggy code release, or inaccurate financial summary is never acceptable.

Verification Mandate: Every team member is required to thoroughly review, fact-check, test, and validate all AI-generated text, calculations, and code snippets before incorporating them into presentations, client deliverables, architectural designs, or software repositories.


################################################################################
5. APPROVED USE CASES FOR GENERATIVE AI ACROSS FUNCTIONAL DOMAINS
################################################################################

When utilizing approved enterprise generative AI tools with non-sensitive data, the following activities are encouraged:

- Software Engineering: Generating boilerplate unit test stubs, refactoring approved code modules for readability, drafting inline code documentation, and exploring alternative algorithm implementations in sandboxed environments.

- Business Operations & Communications: Drafting routine internal announcements, structuring slide presentations, summarizing public industry whitepapers, and refining grammar and readability.

- Customer Support & Documentation: Drafting initial candidate responses for customer help tickets (subject to mandatory human review prior to sending), authoring user troubleshooting manuals, and organizing FAQ knowledge bases.

- Data Analysis & Research: Brainstorming exploratory analytical queries, structuring SQL query syntax against public schemas, and summarizing publicly available academic literature.


################################################################################
6. ENGINEERING AND CODE GENERATION DIRECTIVES
################################################################################

Software engineers utilizing AI-assisted development tools (e.g., Copilot Enterprise) must adhere to specialized technical guardrails:

- Public Code Snippet Filtering: All IDE code assistant plugins must have 'Block Public Code Suggestions' permanently enabled to prevent accidental ingestion of GPL, AGPL, or restrictive copyleft licensed source code.

- Static Code Analysis and Security Scans: All AI-generated code snippets must pass automated Static Application Security Testing (SAST), software composition analysis (SCA), and unit test validation before being committed to version control.

- Architectural Security: AI assistants must never be asked to generate cryptographic routines, key generation logic, or authentication bypasses. Standard, verified internal libraries must be used exclusively.

- Peer Code Review: AI-generated pull requests must undergo identical peer review scrutiny as human-authored code, with the submitting engineer explicitly noting AI assistance in the PR description.


################################################################################
7. PROHIBITED USE CASES AND MALICIOUS ACTIVITIES
################################################################################

The following activities are strictly prohibited and constitute severe policy violations:

1. Generating deepfakes, synthetic voice clones, or deceptive media representing colleagues, executive leaders, or external clients.

2. Utilizing generative AI to author phishing emails, social engineering scripts, or malware payloads.

3. Generating offensive, harassing, discriminatory, sexually explicit, or politically partisan material.

4. Generating automated performance evaluations, disciplinary write-ups, or hiring rejection decisions without qualified human authorship (violating HR-006 and AIG-005).

5. Presenting AI-generated text as original academic or patent authorship without explicit disclosure.


################################################################################
8. PROMPT ENGINEERING AND DATA HYGIENE BEST PRACTICES
################################################################################

Employees are expected to practice safe and structured prompt engineering:

- Contextual Sanitization: Ensure prompts contain only the minimum necessary background data to accomplish the task, scrubbing real customer names, server IP addresses, or proprietary tokens.

- Role and Boundary Specification: Frame prompts clearly with explicit boundaries: e.g., 'Act as a technical documentation editor. Refactor the following sanitized text for grammatical clarity without altering technical meaning.'

- Fact Verification Checks: Cross-reference all citations, library names, and factual claims against authoritative internal or public documentation before executing code or publishing text.

- Context Isolation: Do not combine disparate operational tasks in a single conversational thread to avoid cross-context data bleeding.


################################################################################
9. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY, ATTRIBUTION, AND SHADOW AI ENFORCEMENT
################################################################################

Employees must exercise strict care regarding intellectual property and tool compliance:

- Attribution Disclosures: Where AI tools generate substantive portions of internal research documents or whitepapers, the author must include a brief attribution disclosure note: e.g., 'Drafting assistance provided by Enterprise AI Assistant; verified and edited by [Employee Name].'

- Patent Disclosures: Inventions conceived with AI assistance must be disclosed fully to the Legal Intellectual Property team to verify patent eligibility.

- Shadow AI Prohibitions: Unauthorized use of unsanctioned browser extensions, unapproved consumer LLM wrappers, or personal AI credentials on corporate hardware is strictly blocked by endpoint DLP and CASB firewalls.

- Disciplinary Escalation: Deliberate bypass of enterprise AI controls, unauthorized upload of CONFIDENTIAL data, or failure to verify AI outputs resulting in material harm will trigger immediate disciplinary action under HR-008.


################################################################################
10. CLIENT COMMUNICATIONS, AUTOMATED DISCLAIMERS, AND TRANSPARENCY PROTOCOLS
################################################################################

When generative AI systems draft external client correspondence, technical proposals, or customer support responses, enterprise transparency protocols must be enforced:

- Client Transparency Mandate: Clients possess a legal and ethical right to know when conversational interfaces are powered by artificial intelligence. Automated customer portals must display clear, unambiguous notices stating: 'You are interacting with the Threshold Enterprise AI Virtual Assistant.'

- Human Escalation Escape Hatch: Every customer-facing conversational agent must provide a conspicuous, one-click mechanism allowing the customer to transition immediately to a live human support specialist.

- External Publication Disclaimers: Marketing collateral, technical whitepapers, or public blog posts containing generative AI synthesis must receive editorial review and include standardized corporate disclaimers regarding content verification.

- Contractual Prohibitions: AI tools must never be utilized to draft binding contract clauses, warranties, or legal liabilities without comprehensive review and endorsement by Threshold Corporate Legal Counsel.


################################################################################
11. PROMPT REPOSITORY MANAGEMENT AND COLLABORATIVE KNOWLEDGE ASSETS
################################################################################

To foster reusable, secure, and standardized prompt design patterns across business departments, the Enterprise Governance Office maintains a centralized Prompt Catalog:

- Approved Prompt Library: Departmental power users may submit tested prompt templates (e.g., meeting transcript summarizers, release note formatters) for security review and cataloging in the enterprise prompt repository.

- Versioning and Metadata: Cataloged prompt templates must maintain semantic versioning, documented input/output schemas, target model specifications, and approved data classification levels.

- Periodic Security Auditing: Cataloged prompt templates undergo bi-annual security review to ensure ongoing compliance with prompt injection defense standards under AIG-007.


################################################################################
12. REPORTING CONCERNS, ESCALATION PATHWAYS, AND REFERENCES
################################################################################

If an employee observes a generative AI tool exhibiting dangerous behaviors, outputting sensitive corporate data, generating toxic/hateful content, or attempting unauthorized system actions, the employee must report the incident immediately to ai-incident@threshold-systems.internal in accordance with AIG-009.

Questions regarding whether a specific use case or dataset is permitted should be directed to your departmental HR Business Partner or the Enterprise AI Governance Office.

- AIG-006: AI Data Privacy Policy (Data classification and privacy in AI prompts).
