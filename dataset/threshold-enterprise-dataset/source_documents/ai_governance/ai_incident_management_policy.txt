################################################################################
1. CONFIDENTIAL CLASSIFICATION AND OPERATIONAL PURPOSE
################################################################################

*** CONFIDENTIAL PROCEDURE - RESTRICTED TO TECHNICAL INCIDENT RESPONDERS AND MANAGERS ***

This document establishes the proprietary incident classification criteria, triage workflows, containment playbooks, forensic investigation standards, and recovery runbooks for artificial intelligence incidents at Threshold Enterprise Systems. It is classified as CONFIDENTIAL. Access is restricted to authorized roles (AI_ENGINEER, SECURITY_ENGINEER, MANAGER, ADMIN). Workforce members holding the general EMPLOYEE role are not authorized to retrieve this document within the knowledge base.

The purpose of this AI Incident Management Policy is to provide an agile, defensive, and structured operational procedure to rapidly detect, contain, investigate, remediate, and learn from adverse artificial intelligence events, preventing harm to employees, clients, corporate assets, and organizational reputation.

Artificial intelligence incidents possess unique operational characteristics—such as stochastic outputs, distributed model hallucination, automated agent execution cascades, and subtle algorithmic bias—that require specialized response procedures distinct from traditional IT operational failures.


################################################################################
2. TRI-PARTITE INCIDENT SCOPE: AI VS. CYBER VS. WORKPLACE INCIDENTS
################################################################################

Threshold Enterprise Systems strictly delineates the jurisdictional boundaries among three categories of corporate incidents to ensure rapid routing and avoid operational duplication:

1. Artificial Intelligence Incident (AIG-009): An adverse event caused directly by or involving the operation, failure, manipulation, or hallucination of an artificial intelligence model, RAG system, or autonomous agent. Examples include toxic content emission, prompt injection exploitation, systemic algorithmic bias, severe factual hallucinations in customer communications, or autonomous agent tool runaway.

2. Cybersecurity Incident (SEC-004): An adverse event involving the unauthorized access, compromise, destruction, or denial of traditional IT infrastructure, networks, databases, or privileged credentials. If an AI incident involves an external attacker compromising cloud servers or exfiltrating data, it is co-managed jointly under AIG-009 and SEC-004.

3. Workplace / HR Incident (HR-008): An adverse event involving human interpersonal misconduct, workplace harassment, or intentional employee policy violations. If an employee uses an AI tool to generate harassing material targeting a colleague, the matter is handled under HR-008 with technical evidence supplied under AIG-009.


################################################################################
3. THE END-TO-END DEFENSIVE AI INCIDENT LIFECYCLE
################################################################################

All reported AI incidents must progress through seven rigorous, sequential response phases:

Phase 1 - Detection: The incident is identified through automated monitoring alerts (AIG-008), employee reports, customer support tickets, or red-team vulnerability discoveries.

Phase 2 - Triage: The On-Call AI Incident Commander validates the report, assigns an initial severity classification (Severity 1 to Severity 4), and initiates the incident bridge.

Phase 3 - Risk Assessment: Responders evaluate immediate exposure, affected user cohorts, data classification tiers (SEC-005), and potential regulatory reporting obligations.

Phase 4 - Containment: Immediate tactical actions are executed to isolate the failure, such as model traffic throttling, parameter clamping, prompt filter updates, or master kill-switch activation.

Phase 5 - Investigation: Forensic analysis reconstructs the failure sequence, auditing input prompts, model weights, RAG retrieved chunks, and system execution logs.

Phase 6 - Recovery: The root cause is patched, validation tests are verified in staging, and service is restored through phased canary deployment.

Phase 7 - Post-Incident Review: A formal blameless post-mortem is conducted within five (5) business days, cataloging corrective actions and retraining requirements.


################################################################################
4. FOUR-TIER AI INCIDENT SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION MATRIX
################################################################################

Severity levels govern response velocity, escalation paths, and operational resource allocation:

- Severity 1 (Critical AI Disaster): Autonomous runaway agent causing destructive system modifications; critical data exfiltration of RESTRICTED assets via prompt extraction; severe public-facing toxic/defamatory model output causing massive reputational damage; or safety-critical failure. Response SLA: 15 minutes. Escalation: CISO, Chief AI Officer, CEO, Legal Counsel.

- Severity 2 (Major AI Incident): Widespread hallucination affecting commercial customer deliverables; successful prompt injection bypassing safety guardrails on CONFIDENTIAL internal systems; severe demographic bias detected in production HR scoring; or model outage on core customer-facing systems. Response SLA: 30 minutes. Escalation: Lead AI Engineer, Security Operations Manager, Department VP.

- Severity 3 (Moderate AI Incident): Localized model output anomalies detected by internal staff; isolated retrieval failures in internal RAG pipelines; minor drift exceeding warning thresholds; or intermittent API latency spikes. Response SLA: 2 hours. Escalation: Squad AI Lead, Primary On-Call Engineer.

- Severity 4 (Minor AI Anomaly): Cosmetic formatting errors, isolated user feedback complaints regarding tone, or minor documentation discrepancies. Response SLA: 24 hours. Escalation: Assigned Jira ticket in engineering backlog.


################################################################################
5. EMERGENCY TECHNICAL CONTAINMENT PLAYBOOKS
################################################################################

Incident responders possess pre-authorized technical mechanisms to immediately contain active AI incidents:

Playbook A - Master Kill-Switch Activation: If an AI model or autonomous agent behaves unpredictably, the Incident Commander executes the master kill-switch command in the deployment console. This immediately cuts production traffic, terminates running container pods, and routes traffic to deterministic static fallback error pages within thirty (30) seconds.

Playbook B - Parameter and Guardrail Clamping: For generative models exhibiting hallucination or prompt injection susceptibility, responders dynamically tighten safety filters—lowering temperature to 0.0, injecting emergency prompt prefix constraints, and activating aggressive semantic similarity blocking.

Playbook C - Vector Store Partition Quarantine: For RAG systems where poisoned documents or unauthorized data leakage occurs, responders isolate the affected vector partition, dropping the index from query routing until sanitized.

Playbook D - Rollback to Previous Stable Artifact: Responders initiate an automated rollback to the last verified stable model artifact and container image under AIG-010.


################################################################################
6. FORENSIC ANALYSIS AND ROOT CAUSE INVESTIGATION
################################################################################

Once contained, the AI Engineering and Security forensics team reconstructs the incident:

- Prompt and Completion Log Extraction: Immutable SIEM logs are queried to extract the exact prompt string, system instructions, temperature settings, and raw completion tokens.

- RAG Vector Retrieval Audit: For RAG incidents, the forensic team inspects the exact chunk IDs retrieved, their similarity scores, metadata headers, and source document hashes.

- Model Weight Verification: Cryptographic checksums of deployed model weights are verified against the secure model registry to rule out unauthorized parameter tampering.

- Adversarial Reproduction: The failure scenario is replicated in an isolated staging sandbox to confirm the technical trigger and validate candidate patches.


################################################################################
7. FORENSIC CHAIN OF CUSTODY AND EVIDENCE INTEGRITY
################################################################################

To ensure forensic evidence remains legally admissible and audit-compliant during external regulatory investigations, responders must enforce strict chain of custody protocols:

- Cryptographic Snapshots: Memory dumps, prompt logs, and model state buffers captured during incident triage must be immediately signed with SHA-256 hashes and archived in write-once-read-many (WORM) cloud storage.

- Access Restriction: Forensic evidence folders are restricted strictly to designated Security and AI Forensics Investigators under dual-custody access locks.

- Preservation Timelines: Incident forensic evidence dossiers must be retained for a minimum of seven (7) years in compliance with enterprise data retention schedules.


################################################################################
8. RECOVERY, VALIDATION, AND PHASED RESTORATION
################################################################################

Before an incident is declared resolved and normal operations resume:

- Fix Verification: The remediation (model patch, prompt firewall rule, or sanitized dataset) must pass 100% of standard regression test suites plus dedicated adversarial stress tests.

- Staged Canary Re-Introduction: The repaired model is deployed to 5% of production traffic for a 24-hour soak period under active real-time monitoring before full traffic restoration.

- Formal Clearance: For Severity 1 and Severity 2 incidents, written clearance from the Lead AI Engineer and Information Security Lead is required prior to 100% production traffic promotion.


################################################################################
9. REGULATORY DISCLOSURES, CUSTOMER NOTIFICATIONS, AND TABLETOP DRILLS
################################################################################

Enterprise AI incidents that impact external clients or involve regulatory non-compliance trigger dedicated communication and governance procedures:

- Statutory Reporting Timelines: Under GDPR, CPRA, and the DPDP Act 2023, incidents resulting in personal data exposure through AI completion leaks must be reported to data protection supervisory authorities within seventy-two (72) hours of verification.

- Customer Remediation Communications: External clients affected by AI hallucinations or service disruptions must receive an approved Incident Briefing detailing the root cause, immediate containment, and preventative measures.

- Bi-Annual Incident Simulation Exercises: The AI Incident Response team conducts bi-annual tabletop simulation drills testing response preparedness against simulated catastrophic scenarios, such as autonomous agent runaway, poisoned RAG knowledge bases, and multi-turn prompt extraction attacks.


################################################################################
10. EXECUTIVE BOARD REPORTING, GOVERNANCE AUDIT TRAIL, AND CROSS-REFERENCES
################################################################################

Within five (5) business days following a Severity 1 or Severity 2 incident, the Incident Commander must publish a comprehensive Post-Incident Review (PIR) detailing timeline, root cause, impact, containment actions, and preventive engineering commitments.

A formal executive briefing is delivered to the Board of Directors Risk Committee and Enterprise AI Ethics and Governance Board summarizing lessons learned and infrastructure improvements.

All incident records, forensic evidence hashes, and post-mortem action items are permanently cataloged in the Enterprise Governance Registry.

This policy operates in direct harmony with related enterprise governance standards:

- SEC-004: Security Incident Response Policy (Cybersecurity incident coordination).

- HR-008: Disciplinary Action Policy (Workplace misconduct investigations).

- AIG-007: Prompt Security Policy (Adversarial prompt incident containment).

- AIG-008: AI Model Monitoring Policy (Detection telemetry and automated alerts).
